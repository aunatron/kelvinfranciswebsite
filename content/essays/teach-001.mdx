Ghana's Cyber Security Authority uses different terms for different actors. Cybersecurity Service Providers are licensed. Cybersecurity Establishments and Cybersecurity Professionals are accredited.

That distinction matters because an individual credential does not transfer to a company. It also does not give a professional unlimited authority to test or access systems. Permission still comes from the owner of the system, defined in writing for a specific scope.

## What the framework establishes

The CSA describes a Cybersecurity Professional as a person accredited to perform a cybersecurity-related professional function under Ghana's Cybersecurity Act. Its published framework covers areas such as vulnerability assessment and penetration testing, digital forensics, managed cybersecurity services, and governance, risk, and compliance.

Accreditation records that the individual passed the Authority's process. It does not certify every future piece of work, guarantee an outcome, or create a corporate accreditation.

## What this dossier claims

This site records an individual Cybersecurity Professional accreditation claim for Kelvin-Francis Peprah. It makes no company licence or company accreditation claim for Aunatron Systems.

The public CSA validator requires a certificate number. That number is not published here, so the site's current-status label remains **subject-attested** rather than independently verified. The official verification route is linked beside the credential for a reader who has the required reference.

Precise wording can feel less impressive than a loose badge. It is still the stronger choice. A credential should describe exactly who it belongs to, what kind of regulatory act it represents, and how its status can be checked.
